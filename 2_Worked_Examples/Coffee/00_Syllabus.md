## COURSE DESCRIPTION

This hands-on beginner course teaches you to brew café-quality pour-over coffee at home and adjust it to your own taste. It's practical and tasting-driven, not chemistry: you'll learn the few levers that actually matter — grind, water, ratio — and how each one changes the cup.

Over three short sessions you go from your first repeatable brew, to tasting and diagnosing what's wrong with a cup, to dialing in a recipe you love and can repeat every morning. Every session is hands-on, low-pressure, and judgment-free — coffee should be fun, not intimidating.

The whole course hangs on one simple model: **grind + water + ratio → extraction → taste.** Everything you learn is just adjusting one of those levers.

## COURSE-LEVEL LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Upon completion, you will be able to:

1. Brew a pour-over coffee using a repeatable recipe (ratio, grind, time).
2. Diagnose a flawed cup (sour, bitter, or weak) from taste and identify the likely cause.
3. Adjust grind, ratio, or technique to improve the next cup toward your taste.
4. Explain how grind size, water temperature, and ratio affect extraction — intuitively.
5. Select fresh beans and store them properly.

## MATERIALS

Bring (or have at home): a pour-over dripper (V60 or Melitta), paper filters, a kettle (gooseneck ideal), a kitchen scale, a burr grinder, a timer or phone, and fresh whole-bean coffee. A short pre-class video on grind size and bean freshness is provided before Session 1.

## ASSESSMENT

There are no grades. You gauge your progress two ways: a **tasting self-assessment** (sour / bitter / balanced) you use in every session, and a **brew log** you keep for a week after Session 1. Success means a cup that hits the reference brew in class and a recipe you can repeat at home.

## ASSIGNMENTS

- **Before Session 1:** watch the short grind/freshness video; inspect your own beans (roast date? whole bean?).
- **In session:** follow the brew-along demo, complete a paired brew, and taste-and-compare.
- **Between sessions:** keep your brew log — brew daily, note ratio/grind/taste, and bring questions.
