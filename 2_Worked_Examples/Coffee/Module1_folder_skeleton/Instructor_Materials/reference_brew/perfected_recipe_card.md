# Reference Brew — the instructor's dialed-in cup  — FULLY SHOWN

> **This is the "complete solution" for a non-code course** (Guide §6a, step 2). Every student-facing practice file in this module is degraded *from* this one — the paired fill-in card blanks its decisions, the two-cup experiment varies one of its variables. That's why it exists, and why it's worth getting exactly right before you build anything else.
>
> For a hands-on course, "complete" is **three things together**: the sheet with every number and a one-line *why*, the thing itself done right and left where students can measure against it, and your own dated result.

---

## 1. The sheet — every number, and why it's that number

| Variable | The value | Why this value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dose** | 22 g whole bean, weighed | Fills a V60-02 without crowding the bed; big enough that a 1 g scale error doesn't matter much |
| **Water** | 352 g, filtered | 22 g at **1:16** — the middle of the sweet spot, strong enough to taste structure, not so strong it hides mistakes |
| **Ratio** | **1:16** | The number students leave with. Easy mental math (dose × 16) and forgiving of grind error |
| **Grind** | Medium, table-salt texture | Coarse enough that a beginner's slow pour won't over-extract; fine enough to finish in time |
| **Water temp** | ~200 °F / just off the boil (30 s off heat) | Hot enough to extract sweetness, below boiling so it doesn't scorch and turn bitter |
| **Bloom** | 45 g water, 45 s | About 2× the dose. Lets CO₂ escape so the water can actually reach the grounds |
| **Pour** | Slow circles, finish pouring by 2:00 | Keeps the bed even; pouring too fast channels and leaves dry grounds |
| **Total time** | **3:00–3:30**, drawdown complete | The grind check: much faster means too coarse, much slower means too fine |
| **Target** | Sweet, clean, no bitterness in the finish | What they're aiming at — "good" has to be tasteable, not theoretical |

**Rinse the filter first** (hot water, discard) — removes the papery taste and warms the dripper. Students who skip it taste cardboard and blame the beans.

---

## 2. The thing itself — brewed, and left where students can measure against it

Brew this cup **before class** and leave it on the bench beside the setup, labelled. It is the benchmark the whole module compares to:

- Objective CO1's evidence is *"judged against the reference brew"* — this is that reference. Without a cup on the bench, "judged against" has nothing to point at.
- Students taste it **first**, before they brew anything, so "good" is a memory in their mouth rather than a description in a handout.
- Leave the **used bed** in the dripper too. A flat, evenly-extracted bed is what theirs should look like; a cratered one is the most common tell that they poured too fast.

*If the room can't support a pre-brewed cup, brew it live as segment 1's demo — but then it must be the first thing that happens, not something they see after their own attempt.*

## 3. Your own dated result — the proof it reproduces

| Date | Bean / roast date | Grind setting | Time | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-03-14 | Ethiopian washed, roasted 2026-03-06 | Baratza Encore 18 | 3:12 | Sweet, clean. The target cup. |
| 2026-03-21 | Same bean, roasted 2026-03-18 | Encore 18 | 2:48 | Fresher beans bloomed harder; ran fast. Went to 16 to pull it back. |

**Why record this:** it is how you find out your recipe is drifting before a room of students does. A "complete solution" that worked once isn't complete — complete means *reproducible*, and the dated log is the only evidence of that. It also gives you the honest answer when a student's cup runs fast: *"mine did too, the week the beans were that fresh."*
