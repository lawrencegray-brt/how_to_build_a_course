# Home Coffee Brewing — Design Decisions (Lean)

*Built following Guide §1. One page; the choices everything downstream inherits.*

- **Audience:** Adults who drink coffee and want it better at home. No barista/professional background. They own or will buy a basic pour-over kit. Possibly intimidated by "coffee snob" culture.
- **Objective:** Production — by the end they can **brew a good, repeatable pour-over and adjust it to their taste.** Not the chemistry of extraction.
- **Depth:** Only as deep as needed to brew and fix a cup. Extraction is taught as intuition (grind/temp/time → strength & taste), never as chemistry. Stop where going deeper wouldn't improve their cup.
- **Scope — in:** bean freshness & selection, grind size, coffee-to-water ratio, water temp, the pour technique, tasting, and adjusting. **Out:** espresso, milk drinks, latte art, roasting, commercial gear.
- **Values:** *Humanity* — low-shame throughout; a bad cup is data, not failure. *Quality* — there is a reference brew on the bench, so "good" is something you can taste, not an opinion. *Curiosity* — every session ends with one thing to go try and change.
- **Tone:** Welcoming, encouraging, low-shame. Coffee is intimidating to beginners; normalize mistakes and "I'm still dialing it in." Guidance reminds students of *why* a step matters; never condescends.
- **Tools:** Pour-over dripper (V60/Melitta), paper filters, kettle (gooseneck ideal), kitchen scale, burr grinder, timer/phone.
