# Home Coffee Brewing — Learning Objectives

*Built following Guide §2 + Appendix A. Bloom-tagged, student-focused, measurable. Module objectives ladder up to course objectives.*

## Course-level objectives
By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. **(Apply)** Brew a pour-over coffee using a repeatable recipe (ratio, grind, time). — *Evidence:* in session, brew a cup that hits your target ratio and time, judged against the reference brew.
2. **(Analyze)** Diagnose a flawed cup (sour, bitter, or weak) from taste and identify the likely cause. — *Evidence:* taste a flawed cup and correctly name the likely cause.
3. **(Evaluate)** Adjust grind, ratio, or technique to improve the next cup toward their own taste. — *Evidence:* given a cup that's off, change one variable and brew a better next cup.
4. **(Understand)** Explain how grind size, water temperature, and ratio affect extraction — intuitively. — *Evidence:* in plain words, explain how grind / temp / ratio push a cup's strength and taste (no chemistry).
5. **(Apply)** Select fresh beans and store them properly. — *Evidence:* pick a fresh bag (roast date, whole bean) and say how you'll store it.

## Module 1 objectives — "The Setup & Your First Brew" (→ CO1, CO5)
By the end of Module 1, students will be able to:

1. **(Apply)** Assemble the pour-over setup and measure a coffee-to-water ratio by weight. *(→CO1)*
2. **(Apply)** Grind beans to a medium pour-over grind and rinse the filter. *(→CO1)*
3. **(Apply)** Execute a guided pour-over from bloom to finish, on time. *(→CO1)*
4. **(Apply)** Identify a fresh bean (roast date, whole bean) and store it correctly. *(→CO5)*

*Note: CO4 is an **(Understand)**-level objective on purpose — the audience needs the intuition, not mastery of extraction science. Aim high where the content supports it; don't force a higher verb than the goal needs.*
