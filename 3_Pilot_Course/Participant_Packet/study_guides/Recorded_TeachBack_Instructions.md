# Recording Your Teach-Back

*How the course ends. Two things are due together, two weeks after Session 5: your module, built to the floor, and a ten-minute teach-back of one segment of it, recorded. The instructor reviews the recording; the folder rows of the rubric are your own checklist. You get the feedback form back the week after.*

## 1. Build first

Build your one module to the **minimum-viable floor** with the production loop — five pieces, each a file in the folder you laid out: the **complete version, verified** (the full worked example you teach from, run or done so you know it works) · **one practice derived from it** (the same thing with the decisions taken out) · **a pre-class study guide** (the reading and warm-up that get students in primed) · **one segment of your teaching guide** (the script for one piece of the live session) · **a self-check** (the short quiz students use on themselves, never a grade). **If that is more than the time allows, the minimum that counts is one segment, complete** — its script in your teaching guide, and the complete version it narrates. Keep the AI-direction + verification log as you go (the template is in your fill-in file).

## 2. Before you record

- Run **AI-9.1** (the Directing AI card): *"Here is my Decisions doc and my module. You are one of the students it describes, and it didn't work for you. You're not hostile, just honest. What went wrong?"* Treat what comes back as things to watch for — never findings.
- Pick **two** of the eight *Teaching It Well* habits to try: show the whole picture first · small sure steps · an analogy or a story · draw it live · go slow where it matters · teach warm · ask, don't just tell · leave them wanting the next room.

## 3. Record ten minutes

- **One segment**, taught from **your own teaching guide**, exactly as you would teach it in your real course. Ten minutes; a little under is fine.
- Any recorder you have — Teams, Zoom, Loom, your phone. Camera optional. Share your screen if the segment shows something (a notebook, a diagram, a sheet).
- Teach to a student, not to the reviewer. Do not stop to explain your choices; the guide and the retro do that.

## 4. Watch it back once — this is your retro, for you

With your guide open beside the video, before you send it:

1. **Time it from the timestamp**, not from memory, and compare it to the estimate on your agenda.
2. **Mark every moment you said something that is in your head and not on the page.** That gap is where a course dies when you leave the room; **add each one to your guide now**, before you send it.
3. **Note what you would change**, and where it lands — which MDD row or module file. Nothing is written up for the instructor; the recording and the guide you send are the result.

Then, if you like, **AI-9.2**: *"Here are my notes from the run. Turn them into specific changes, each naming the MDD row or module file it lands in. Don't propose anything my notes don't support."*

## 5. Send, by the date given in class

- the recording (a link or a file)
- the segment of your teaching guide you taught from
- your module folder, as a zip (your evaluate-and-improve plan is in the MDD; your AI log is in the folder)
- **Rubric 5.3's folder rows, ticked by you** — the artifact review is your own checklist; the instructor confirms at a glance that the things are there and does not read the module

## 6. What comes back

The **feedback form** — the teach-back half of Rubric 5.3 filled in by the instructor from your recording, with a timestamp at each head-not-page moment; your own ticks on the folder rows, confirmed; whether Deliverable C is complete or the one sentence that would complete it; and one strength and one thing to sharpen, each tied to a rubric row. Met or not yet — nothing is scored.

*Optional, for a later cohort or if you want it: swap recordings with one peer, watch theirs with their guide segment open, and send two lines — a strength and a sharpen, each naming a rubric row — plus one head-not-page timestamp.*
