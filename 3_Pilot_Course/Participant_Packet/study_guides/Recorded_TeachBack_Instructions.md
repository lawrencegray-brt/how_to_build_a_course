# Recording Your Teach-Back

*How the course ends. Two things are due together, two weeks after Session 5: your module, built to the floor, and a ten-minute teach-back of one segment of it, recorded. You get the feedback form back — the module rubric, filled in — the week after.*

## 1. Build first

Build your one module to the **minimum-viable floor** with the production loop: a verified complete version · one practice derived from it · a pre-class study guide · one teaching-guide segment · a self-check. **If that is more than the time allows, the minimum that counts is one segment, complete** — its script in your teaching guide, and the complete version it narrates. Keep the AI-direction + verification log as you go (the template is in your fill-in file).

## 2. Before you record

- Run **AI-9.1** (the Directing AI card): *"Here is my Decisions doc and my module. You are one of the students it describes, and it didn't work for you. You're not hostile, just honest. What went wrong?"* Treat what comes back as things to watch for — never findings.
- Pick **two** of the eight *Teaching It Well* habits to try: show the whole picture first · small sure steps · an analogy or a story · draw it live · go slow where it matters · teach warm · ask, don't just tell · leave them wanting the next room.

## 3. Record ten minutes

- **One segment**, taught from **your own teaching guide**, exactly as you would teach it in your real course. Ten minutes; a little under is fine.
- Any recorder you have — Teams, Zoom, Loom, your phone. Camera optional. Share your screen if the segment shows something (a notebook, a diagram, a sheet).
- Teach to a student, not to the reviewer. Do not stop to explain your choices; the guide and the retro do that.

## 4. Watch it back once — this is your retro

With your guide open beside the video:

1. **Time it from the timestamp**, not from memory, and write down how it compared to the estimate on your agenda.
2. **Mark every moment you said something that is in your head and not on the page.** That gap is where a course dies when you leave the room; each one is a line to add to the guide.
3. **Write what you would change**, and where it lands — which MDD row or module file. Half a page is enough.

Then, if you like, **AI-9.2**: *"Here are my notes from the run. Turn them into specific changes, each naming the MDD row or module file it lands in. Don't propose anything my notes don't support."*

## 5. Send, by the date given in class

- the recording (a link or a file)
- the segment of your teaching guide you taught from
- your half-page retro
- your evaluate-and-improve plan (written in Session 5; it goes in your MDD as a short note)
- your AI-direction + verification log
- your module folder, as a zip

## 6. What comes back

The **feedback form** — Rubric 5.3 filled in: the artifact rows from your folder, the teach-back rows from the recording with a timestamp at each head-not-page moment, your retro and plan, whether Deliverable C is complete or the one sentence that would complete it, and one strength and one thing to sharpen, each tied to a rubric row. Met or not yet — nothing is scored.

*Optional, for a later cohort or if you want it: swap recordings with one peer, watch theirs with their guide segment open, and send two lines — a strength and a sharpen, each naming a rubric row — plus one head-not-page timestamp.*
