## PROGRAM DESCRIPTION

This five-week pilot teaches subject-matter experts a repeatable method for building high-quality courses and training — the same method behind the program's flagship courses. It is hands-on and production-focused: you bring a real subject you want to teach, and you leave with a real course-in-progress, not notes about course design.

Through instructor-led sessions and guided production between meetings, you will turn a subject into measurable learning objectives, a Master Design Document, and one fully-built module — applying values-first design, Bloom's taxonomy, research-based teaching practices, and GenAI production under guardrails. No prior course-design experience is required.

The pilot mirrors the method it teaches: each module is delivered in the same three-package structure (instructor materials, student materials, a portable teaching guide) you are learning to build, so you experience the method while learning it.

## COURSE-LEVEL LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Upon successful completion, you will be able to:

1. Write course- and module-level learning objectives using Bloom's measurable verbs.
2. Audit a course design against the seven research-based teaching practices to find gaps.
3. Produce a complete Master Design Document for a course.
4. Build one fully-realized module across all three packages (instructor, student, teaching guide).
5. Direct GenAI to draft materials and evaluate its output against guardrails for correctness and tone.
6. Embed your organization's values into course activities and assessments.
7. Apply accessibility (UDL) principles so your course works for diverse learners.
8. Plan how to evaluate a delivered course and improve it for the next run.

## ASSESSMENT

This pilot is assessed on a **completed / not-completed basis — there are no letter grades.** You will receive constructive feedback on every deliverable. Success means producing the three deliverables to the standard of the rubrics (shared in Week 1) and a module you could genuinely hand to someone else to teach.

**The three deliverables:**

- **A — Learning Objectives** (Weeks 1–2): course objectives first (Week 1), then the module objectives that ladder up to them, written once you decompose into modules (Week 2).
- **B — Master Design Document** (Week 2): every module mapped — objectives, topics, materials, activities, values.
- **C — One complete module, built and taught** (Weeks 3–5): structured (Wk 3), produced with GenAI (Wk 4), and taught as a **10-minute recorded teach-back** of one segment, submitted with the module folder two weeks after Session 5 and returned with instructor feedback on the module rubric. The minimum that counts is one segment, complete.

## ASSIGNMENTS

- **Pre-work** — short reading/preparation before each session so live time is spent producing, not lecturing. Includes coming to Session 1 with the subject you want to build a course on.
- **Live sessions** — five required 2-hour Zoom meetings of instruction, guided building, and feedback. Attendance expected.
- **Between-session production** — the real work: drafting your objectives, MDD, and module. In Week 4 you also run a **Review Council** review of your module and revise, keeping a short AI-direction + verification log.
- **Teach-back** — you record a 10-minute segment of your module, taught from your own teaching guide, watch it back once and write a half-page retro, and submit both with your module folder. Feedback is from the instructor, on Rubric 5.3 — met / not yet, no scores. Peer feedback is optional and was not used in the first cohort.
